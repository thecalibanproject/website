## Platform

web

## Users
- **Enterprise buyers** (primary): CTOs and heads of data or security at companies that cannot send their data to SaaS AI. They are regulated, run data warehouses on MongoDB or SQL, and need on-prem or VPC deployment. Their job is to judge whether Caliban can bring AI to their data safely, correctly and under their control.
- **Developers**: engineers who point an existing OpenAI or Anthropic SDK at Caliban, connect datasources and build nodes. Their job is to see the API, SDKs and integration path quickly.
- **Investors and partners**: people judging the company's technical depth and how defensible the architecture is.

## Product Purpose
Caliban is a sovereign AI gateway and the single AI endpoint for a company. It connects the company's datasources through a governed ontology, classifies intent and routes each request to the right model and agent, pseudonymizes personal data before anything leaves the trust boundary, cuts tokens and latency through caching and routing, and runs 100% on the customer's own hardware. Success for the site: a qualified buyer or developer understands that Caliban brings models to their data and asks to talk.

## Positioning
**Bring models to your data.** Caliban lives on the customer's island: the AI comes to the data, governed by the company's own ontology and keys, instead of the data going out to AI. The mechanisms that back this claim:
- A typed query IR. The LLM picks ontology elements, and a deterministic Rust compiler writes linted MongoDB pipelines or SQL over a change-stream replica. Both lanes return the same rows, and this is tested.
- BYOK only. Upstream credentials are never pooled.
- Zero-egress operation with open-weight models.
- One binary that runs as SaaS, in a VPC or air-gapped.

## Operating Context
Buyers evaluate through architecture review, security questionnaires, published research and a technical demo. Developers evaluate through API docs, SDK snippets and the OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible endpoints. The website is also the public architecture reference. Content comes from `../docs` (the reference architecture and research notes 01–09) and `../core` (OpenAPI, config, compiler tests).

## Capabilities and Constraints
- **Built and tested:**
  - The OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible data plane: chat, embeddings and rerank.
  - The PII pipeline: patterns plus a multilingual NER model, realistic surrogates, and streaming rehydration.
  - Routing with shared on-prem model pools (Qwen, gpt-oss, …).
  - The exact cache with per-tenant isolation.
  - Rate limits and token quotas, and OTel tracing that records no content.
  - The Postgres control plane with a hash-chained audit log and signed snapshots for split routers.
  - The MongoDB connector, the CDC replica and the CQIR compiler (native and replica lanes give the same answers).
  - The web console, SDKs (TS and Python), deploy (compose, Helm, air-gap bundle), and the Docker image.
- **In progress or planned:**
  - The semantic cache.
  - Executing nodes and agents (the spec and budgets are done).
  - MCP/A2A exposure.
  - A shared Valkey quota store.
  - Tenant-scoped surrogates for cache hits.
- **Never claim:** certifications (GDPR, SOC 2, HIPAA are "designed to support, not certified"), customer names, benchmark numbers Caliban did not measure. Published research numbers must be attributed and linked.

## Product Principles
1. Bring the model to the data, never the data to the model.
2. Determinism around the model: code compiles, secures and validates; the model makes bounded choices.
3. Sovereign by construction: BYOK, zero egress, customer-held keys, verifiable audit.
4. Honest evidence: show what is built, label what is target or planned, cite all research.
5. One endpoint, any model: open weights are first-class, not a fallback.
